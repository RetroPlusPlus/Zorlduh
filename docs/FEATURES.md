# Features

One row per feature. A feature's document, when it has one, is `docs/features/<name>.md`, named as in
the first column. Status changes are logged in `docs/features/CHANGELOG.md`.

**Status:** ⬜ planned · 🟡 in progress · 🧪 testing · ✅ complete · ❌ dropped

| Feature | Status | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| build-system | ✅ complete | CMake with `add_subdirectory(engine)`; `zorlduh-lib`, the `zorlduh` executable, one GoogleTest executable per test file; a Release-by-default, dead-stripped, symbol-stripped binary that opens a 60 Hz Game Boy Color window |
| [ci](features/ci.md) | 🧪 testing | Every push to a `ci/**` branch builds everything and runs every test on five self-hosted runners — Linux x64, Windows x64, macOS ARM64, Linux ARM64, Windows ARM64 — the ARM64 three in series on their shared host; push-only triggers, so no pull request can start a run (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, `ci/runner-setup/`) |
| layer-kit | 🧪 testing | The return types (with `Raster`), `LayerOptions` and `RasterLayerOptions`, and one aggregate per kind of layer content — `SpriteLayer`, `TileLayer`, `RasterLayer`, `ImageLayer`: a header-only module in `modules/LayerKit/` that depends on the engine alone, with its own guide (`modules/LayerKit/docs/`) and a compiled getting-started program |
| assets | 🧪 testing | Placeholder art written by `tools/placeholder_art.py` under `assets/` — 2-bit grayscale index sheets (the overlay, sprite, hearts and glyph sheets' index 0 transparent), palette images, and viewable grayscale tile and collision maps, one pair per area, with a map for each further layer of an area; the glyph sheet of 43 uppercase characters and its text and shadow palettes; the wood and stone border sheets and their palettes; the sign and rock terrain tiles; the plain border sheet and its palette, and the dim and selected text palettes; 65 files — and `zorlduh::loadAssets`, which uploads every sheet and palette, decodes every area's maps and builds the game's font, all baked into the executable |
| world | ❌ dropped | One world map of free-form rooms with a room table, `zorlduh::World` and `WorldTiles`; replaced by one map per area in `world-kit` and `areas`. The depth table and the screen layout it added (`src/render/layers.h`, `layout.h`) stay |
| top-down-movement | 🧪 testing | `TopDownMovement` in `modules/TopDownMovement/`: a `WalkerConfig` (speed, 8-way or 4-way `movement`, 4 or 8 `sprites` directions, collision shape, anchors, a walk animation per sprite direction); a `Walker` bound to a position the game keeps, walked by a heading — a pixel at a time per axis, stopping flush at walls and carrying on along one walked into diagonally — moved by `moveTo`, turned by `face`; an `InputWalker` steered by the pad, keeping the press order; `routeFrom` and `follow` moving a walker along the engine's `SpritePath`, facing along its travel and walking however slowly it goes; collision shapes from boxes, engine shapes or anchors against a `TileCollision` map or any query, `readDirections`, and `WalkerSprite`; header-only, depending on the engine alone, with a getting-started program over art of its own and a guide of eight pages — getting started, concepts, collision, routes, how-to recipes, troubleshooting, testing and the reference |
| player | 🧪 testing | The player as a walker: `PlayerConfig` from the player sheet's four rows, the action map (arrows, WASD, d-pad; interact, attack, start), `GameState`, `Config` and `tick`, and the `Player` component, drawn at each area's actor slot between what lies underfoot and what hangs overhead |
| text-kit | 🧪 testing | `TextKit` in `modules/TextKit/`: a `Font` (a glyph sheet, the character of each slot as a string, a palette, an optional shadow palette, the pen's step) with the case rule — a character drawn by its own glyph or else its other case's — and `glyphOf`; `Text`, a passage as sprites, one per glyph, keyed by character position, laid out in lines, aligned left, center or right within a width, with a shadow sprite beneath each glyph, throwing for a character the font lacks; `wrap` and `measure`; and `Reveal`, a typewriter advanced once per tick; header-only, depending on the engine alone, with its own guide and a compiled getting-started program over a font sheet and palettes of its own |
| area-name | 🧪 testing | The HUD strip's first content: the current area's name (`areaName`), right-aligned across the strip in the game's font with its shadow, typed out by a `Reveal` in `GameState` that starts over on entering an area (`AreaName`, on its own sprite layer at the HUD's depth) |
| window-kit | 🧪 testing | `WindowKit` in `modules/WindowKit/`: a `Border` (a sheet of the nine pieces of a box and an arrow, and its palette), `thickness` and `inside`; `Box`, a rectangle of any pixel size filled and framed by pieces of any size, each piece past the end of its span cut by a region of its own, keyed by its place in the box and the box's size; `Window`, the box beneath a game's sprites on one sprite layer; `Dialog`, a message whose box opens and closes, wrapped and paged to its room, typed out, with a blinking arrow, and a confirm that finishes whatever is in progress; `DialogText`, `DialogArrow` and `DialogBox`; header-only on the engine, LayerKit and TextKit, with its own guide and a compiled getting-started program over art of its own |
| window-animation | 🧪 testing | Windows that open and close over time, in WindowKit: `Animation`, how far open a window is, over a time or a count of ticks along an easing curve, reversible and skippable; five styles drawn from it — `Grow` from any point, `Clip`, `Squash`, `Fade`, `Slide` — with `Style` and `Animate`; a `Window` that takes an animation and shows its contents once open; a `Dialog` that runs its own box's animation |
| signs | 🧪 testing | Things in the world that are read: a wooden sign in the meadow and a stone rock beside the lake's dungeon door, each a solid cell, read with Interact while facing it (`src/game/signs.{h,cpp}`); the message's box opens and closes over 300 ms, growing from its middle for those two, and in the shrine six signs on a grid each open theirs in one of the six styles, saying which; the world holds still while the message is up, and Interact finishes whatever the message is doing — the box opening, the page typing, the page, the box closing; the message box (`Dialog`, `src/render/dialog.{h,cpp}`) across the bottom of the screen above the HUD, in the border of what is read |
| menu-kit | 🧪 testing | `MenuKit` in `modules/MenuKit/`: a `MenuConfig` (a window's box, a grid of any number of columns, the rows showing, pages or scrolling, wrapping, whether cancel closes it, a repeat over a time or a count of ticks, a window animation, and the items — each a label, a value, whether it can be chosen and the menu it opens); a `Menu` that runs the stack of open menus, their cursors, rows showing and windows from one `advance` per tick, reporting what was chosen, refused, moved, opened, cancelled and closed in a `Step`, with `close` and `back`; `configOf`, `itemBox` and `readMenuInput`; a `Look` and `MenuItems`, `MenuCursor` (the border's arrow turned to point right, keyed by its item), `MenuArrows` and `MenuWindows`, one WindowKit window per open menu on a layer of its own; header-only on the engine, LayerKit, TextKit and WindowKit, with its own guide and a compiled getting-started program over art of its own |
| script-kit | 🧪 testing | `ScriptKit` in `modules/ScriptKit/`: scripts written in C++ as data — a `Script` is a rule (a `Trigger` from the game's own enum, criteria, a priority, the scripts it holds still) over a body of entries (`wait` by time or ticks, `waitWhile`, `when` / `orWhen` / `otherwise`, `loop`, `loopWhile`, `set`, `call`, `stop`, `restart`, and the game's commands); typed `Value`s and `Expr`essions over them, checked by the compiler; a `ScriptProcessor` that steps every running script a little each tick in priority order on a budget, with calls, scripts held and carrying on, and each script reading on the tick after it asks; what a script asks for coming out as `Request`s — a `Command` with typed `Arguments`, or a `Change` of a value; `Bindings` and `Handlers` for the game's side, and `applyScripts` and `stepScripts` at the two ends of the tick; an advance that touches only its own processor. A header-only module on the engine alone, with its guide (`modules/ScriptKit/docs/`) and a getting-started program over its own art: a street crossing run by twenty-two scripts, one to a file. The game does not use it yet |
| menus | 🧪 testing | The game's menus (`src/game/menus.{h,cpp}`): a title screen at launch — the game's name and START, CONTINUE (not yet available) and OPTIONS — and a pause menu Start opens in play, with the world held still under it — RESUME, OPTIONS, WARP and TITLE; OPTIONS sets how fast messages type (SLOW, NORMAL, FAST) and how the menus' windows open and close (the six styles, two across and scrolling); WARP lists all seventeen areas five at a time and puts the player at the open place nearest the middle of the one chosen; drawn in a plain border, the item under the cursor in gold and a disabled one in gray (`src/render/menus.{h,cpp}`), above the message box |
| camera-kit | 🧪 testing | `CameraKit` in `modules/CameraKit/`: a `View` (the screen the camera shows and where it sits on a layer), a `Camera` value with `place`, a straight `pan` over N ticks through the engine's tween, and `scroll` for a view, and the `follow` law — center the focus, stop at the bounds' edges, center bounds smaller than the view; header-only, depending on the engine alone, with its own guide and a compiled getting-started program |
| world-kit | 🧪 testing | `WorldKit` in `modules/WorldKit/`: a `World` of named `Layout`s of named `Area`s, a place found by those names through `placeOf` — each area its collision map, its `MapLayer`s (tile maps each with its own art catalog and depth, and `ReservedLayer`s: depths held under a name for layers the game builds itself, of any kind), its named `ActorSlot` depths, and named `Opening`s in walls or at spots, each opening with its `Transition` (scroll, fade or seamless) and where it `leadsTo`, in any layout, with an optional cell offset — checked by `validate`; a `Camera` that hands out the area's solid query (reaching into the next area past a seam), follows a box by the layout's camera law only as the box moves, slides the next area in along the door's axis, fades to the entrance, or switches across a seam with the screen holding still, and hands back the move to apply; `WorldLayers` (a tile layer per map layer of each area shown, confined to the view: an area alone at its declared depths, and the areas on screen spread so every lower declared depth stays under every higher one and each layer has a depth of its own), `Actors` (the game's sprites at the current area's slot), `ActorZ` and `LayerZ` (a slot's or a layer's depth, declared or drawn for an area on screen), `TopZ` (the highest depth any layer is drawn at) and `Fade`; areas drawn through chains of seams, as many seams out as the layout's `seamDepth` allows; header-only on the engine, LayerKit and CameraKit, with its own guide and a compiled getting-started program, a castle and its grounds run by the scene with five inhabitants written in the world; its actors, `actor-kit`, are a second target in the same module; and `Scene`, the world running over them: actors written in an area's, a layout's or the world's `actors` (`AreaActor`, `LayoutActor`, `WorldActor`), each level its `Reach`, spawned into one set; the camera handed over and one actor followed by id or name; each other actor carried through the openings its reach allows by the opening's transition, and at the edge of it stopped, despawned or hidden (`AtEdge`); each actor awake or frozen by its `AwakeWhen` rule or by hand (`SceneActor`); the whole world held still while a scroll or a fade runs; one sprite layer per slot holding the actors of every area on screen in the current area's pixels, so they sort by their feet together and keep their sprites across a seam or a scroll, with a function of the game's own to change or add sprites on their way |
| areas | 🧪 testing | The game's world of three layouts: the overworld of five areas (meadow, lake, field, shrine, cave), each its own map pair, joined by scrolling doors and a fading cave mouth, the lake's door down into `dungeon-one`, and the field's forest path down into `seamless-forest` (`src/game/areas.{h,cpp}`); the player goes between them through the tick, which records `AreaChanged` with the layout and area; every area a ground layer and the `"actors"` slot at depths from the game's depth table, the meadow's cave mouth under rock; the screen is the areas' layers with the actors among them and the fade, under the HUD strip |
| dungeon-one | 🧪 testing | The dungeon layout: five rooms (entrance, hall, west wing, east wing, treasury) with their own art, entered by a fade from the lake; doors that scroll between the entrance, the hall and the wings; the hall's whole north wall open to the treasury as a seam |
| seamless-forest | 🧪 testing | The forest layout: seven rooms (edge, glade, thicket, bower, hollow, brook, clearing) of different sizes, joined only by seams, each pair a case of how the camera follows — equal to the view, bigger, smaller with two seams, seen through another room, a chain shorter than the view, a join two cells lower, a room entered off center — with its own tile sheet and grass and leaf palettes, entered by a scroll from the field; a path under the player's feet in the edge and a band of canopy over the player in the glade |
| [actor-kit](features/actor-kit.md) | 🧪 testing | `ActorKit`, a part of WorldKit in `modules/WorldKit/` (headers in `include/ActorKit/`, the target `ActorKit::ActorKit`): an `Actor` is a plain value — the slot it is drawn at, its box, whether it is solid, the values it holds as its own, its looks chosen by its values, its scripts (constants, or made by a function of the values they act on), what moves it (`movedBy` a mover of the game's type, driven by the pad, a direction value or a function), the actions it performs and the triggers it sets off; an `Actors` value holds every actor spawned from such values, at any time, each with an id of its own, and a game keeps as many as it likes; `tick` writes what the scripts asked for, walks every awake mover against the area's map and the other solid actors (two whose boxes overlap walk apart), held inside what its confinement names, performs actions on the actor faced, works out the triggers — ActorKit's own `Interact`, `Touch`, `Near`, `Enter` and `Becomes`, and any the game names in a `TriggerMap` — and steps each actor's own copy of its scripts; own values beside the game's bound ones and the six ActorKit fills in; `spawn` and `remove` from a script, and `Handlers` handed the asking actor; a `Config` of the world, the game's interact action, its triggers, bindings and handlers; `Sprites`, the sprites of one area's actors at one slot, under keys that stay apart across every `Actors` value, with `fresh` for a move shown at once; `player`, the actor of a set that walks out through its area's openings whatever its confinement and fills in `PlayerHere`, `PlayerX` and `PlayerY` for every actor's scripts; `confinement`, what each other actor's walk is held inside — its area (the default), its layout, or the map's walls alone — so an actor walks out through doors and seams as the player does; `freeze` and `wake`, an actor held exactly as it is, still drawn and still solid, and carried on from there. It reads the WorldKit camera and leaves it as it is. Header-only on the engine, LayerKit, the world and ScriptKit, with a section of WorldKit's README, a guide of ten pages in WorldKit's `docs/actors/` (getting started, concepts, the actors reference, triggers, scripts, movers, drawing, how-to, troubleshooting, testing) and an example program, `WorldKit-example-farm`, over art of its own: a farmyard of three areas, its pond rippling under a custom shader, each area's actors drawn on a layer of that area's own. The game does not use it yet |
